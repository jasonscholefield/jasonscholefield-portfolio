This exploration looked at how clearer UX patterns and information hierarchy could improve usability while preserving the game's hardcore identity.</p>

## The Problem

- Heavy memorization demands, offloaded to notebooks and spreadsheets
- Reliance on third-party wikis just to make a basic loadout decision
- Trial-and-error learning, with real, consequence-heavy stakes
- Dense menus that bury the stats that actually matter
- UI elements that sometimes work against the information they're meant to surface

<Callout eyebrow="Problem to solve" center>
  <p>The opportunity was in improving how information surfaces, not in oversimplifying the underlying systems.</p>
</Callout>

## Strategy & Execution

Two areas carried the clearest, most specific friction: ammunition stats, where the numbers that decide a fight are hard to compare quickly, and scopes, where a purchase decision often came down to a single small thumbnail.

<ContentSplit heading="Ammunition Stats You Can Actually Compare" image={ammoStatsA} imageAlt="An in-game ammo examine screen where the penetration tooltip overlaps the armor damage and fragmentation stats next to it" imagePosition="right">
  <p>Critical stats like penetration, armor damage, and fragmentation chance were often hard to compare at a glance. Here, the tooltip meant to explain penetration ends up covering the two stats next to it, exactly the kind of friction that sends a player to a spreadsheet instead.</p>
</ContentSplit>

<ContentImageFeature heading="Letting Color Replace the Tooltip" image={ammoStatsB} imageAlt="Three color-coded explorations of the armor-penetration table, using color instead of a hover state to show tier effectiveness" preserveAspect>
  <p>Three explorations for surfacing that same tier-by-tier penetration data through color instead of a hover state, so the numbers that matter most are visible immediately, not hidden behind a tooltip.</p>
</ContentImageFeature>

<Callout quote="Hardcore systems still benefit from clarity and hierarchy." center />

<ContentSplit heading="A Single Thumbnail Isn't Enough" image={scopeA} imageAlt="An in-game scope examine screen showing only a single small reticle thumbnail" imagePosition="right">
  <p>Some scopes showed nothing more than a small reticle thumbnail tucked in the corner, not much to judge a purchase by when the alternative was checking a wiki first.</p>
</ContentSplit>

<ContentSplit heading="Comparing Reticles Before You Commit" image={scopeB} imageAlt="An in-game scope examine screen showing two reticle previews side by side for direct comparison" imagePosition="left">
  <p>Other scopes already did this better, letting a player compare reticle options side by side before installing. The idea explored here was making that pattern consistent everywhere, not just on the scopes that happened to support it.</p>
</ContentSplit>
