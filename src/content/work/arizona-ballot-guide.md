## The Problem

Local election research meant jumping between disconnected sources: campaign websites, government databases, news articles, and voter guides. The information existed, but it wasn't connected — leaving voters to answer three basic questions on their own: who is running, what do they actually believe, and how have they voted.

## Strategy & Execution

The guiding principle: help voters spend less time searching and more time understanding. The platform centers on five core functions:

1. Finding ballots by address
2. Browsing candidates by office, city, county, or district
3. Side-by-side candidate comparisons
4. Surfacing issue positions, voting records, endorsements, and sources
5. Providing transparency about information sources and verification dates

The design philosophy prioritized clarity and neutrality over persuasion at every step.

## Outcome

- Searchable candidate and race database
- Address-based ballot lookup
- Comparison experiences built for local, often under-covered races
- Data aggregated from government agencies, campaigns, legislative records, and news
- AI-assisted workflows with human review and source attribution
- A semi-automated data pipeline built to handle inconsistent sources and anti-bot protections

## Impact

The platform turned scattered candidate research into a single destination — letting voters research, compare, understand positions, and trace every claim back to its original source.
