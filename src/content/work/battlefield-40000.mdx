## The Concept

The hypothetical game combines large-scale, squad-based multiplayer with a faction-driven, darker sci-fi setting: Battlefield's combined-arms warfare crossed with Warhammer 40,000's grim, faction-obsessed universe.

Rather than designing a complete game, the project aimed to establish tone, worldbuilding, and suggested gameplay roles, and to create a believable landing-page experience around that speculative framing.

<Callout quote="In the grim darkness of the far future, Battlefield is reborn." center />

## Strategy & Execution

The entire build happened directly in Webflow, with emphasis on page structure and scroll pacing, visual hierarchy across long-form layouts, atmospheric imagery, and section transitions that guide narrative flow. Every faction needed its own page, its own tone, and its own complete breakdown of weapons, vehicles, and tactics, without ever implying one faction was simply stronger than another on paper.

Space Marines, Chaos Space Marines, T'au, and Necrons each got a full page: their own hero art, their own primary and secondary weapons, throwables, melee, and vehicles. Balance stays in the gameplay mechanics, not the lore, so no faction reads as objectively stronger than another.

## The Same Five Roles, Told Differently

Every faction has access to the same five squad roles, but the expression of each one shifts with the faction playing it. A Support player is an Apothecary with healing packs and fast revives for the Space Marines, but a Necron Support instead reanimates fallen warriors. The role stays the same. The tool changes.

Here's that system in practice: the T'au faction page, cycling through all five roles.

## A Reinforcement System Per Faction

Each faction earns its own version of the same resource loop under a different name, spent on reinforcements that fit its own identity: Space Marines call in an Orbital Requisition, Chaos channels Warp Influence, T'au routes a Tactical Command Uplink, and Necrons gather Quantum Resonance.

## Seven Battlefields, Seven Reasons to Fight

Every map got its own history: a Hive World stripped to ferrocrete and plasteel, a disciplined fortress-world under Necron siege, an excavation site hiding a Necron Tomb World beneath it. None of them are just a backdrop. Each one explains why the fight is happening there.
