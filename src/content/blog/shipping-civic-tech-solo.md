Working on a product team, scope creep gets absorbed. Someone else picks up the extra work, a sprint slips a little, and the cost is spread thin enough that nobody really feels it. Building Arizona Ballot Guide alone removed that cushion entirely. Every feature I added was time I wasn't spending on the data pipeline, the design, or just making the thing work correctly.

## Constraints as a design tool

Without a team to absorb complexity, I found myself asking a much sharper version of "should we build this?" The real question wasn't "is this valuable," but "is this valuable enough to be the only thing I do this week?" That question killed a lot of features that would have shipped fine on a normal team, and it's a better question than the one most teams actually ask.

## The parts that couldn't be cut

Some things weren't negotiable regardless of solo constraints: source attribution, neutrality in how candidates were presented, and data accuracy. Civic tools live or die on trust, and trust doesn't get a "v2." Everything else (comparison UI polish, additional filters, a more elaborate onboarding flow) got cut or simplified without much hesitation.
