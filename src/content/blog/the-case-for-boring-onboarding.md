Onboarding is one of the few places in a product where the design team gets real license to be creative: animated walkthroughs, progressive reveals, celebratory moments. It's also one of the few places where that creativity most often works against the user.

## What onboarding is actually for

An onboarding flow isn't a chance to showcase the product's personality. It's a bridge between "I just signed up" and "I know what to do next." Every screen that isn't directly closing that gap is a screen the user has to get through before they can do the thing they came to do.

## Boring, on purpose

The onboarding flows that performed best in testing were the least visually ambitious: a short, linear sequence, one decision per screen, and an exit as soon as the user had enough context to be dangerous. No confetti, no mandatory tour of every feature. Just enough friction to orient, and no more.

That doesn't mean onboarding can't have personality. Voice and tone still matter. But the structure underneath should be almost invisible. If someone can describe your onboarding flow in detail after using the product for a week, it probably took longer than it needed to.
